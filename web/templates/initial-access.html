🔓 What is Initial Access?

Initial Access means the attacker gets their first entry point into a computer system or organization.

💡 Simple example: Someone is standing outside a building. The building has a locked door.

If that person somehow gets through the door, they now have initial access.

In cybersecurity, the "door" could be a compromised account, vulnerable service, malicious attachment, stolen credentials, or another entry point.

🏥 Healthcare Example

Imagine a hospital employee receives a fake message pretending to be from the hospital's IT department.

The employee interacts with the message and the attacker manages to obtain an entry point.

The attacker may then attempt to move further through the environment and reach additional systems.

This is why the first entry point is extremely important to detect quickly.

🛡️ How Can Defenders Stop Initial Access?

✓ Train employees to identify phishing messages
✓ Use multi-factor authentication (MFA)
✓ Use email security and spam filtering
✓ Monitor unusual account activity
✓ Keep operating systems and software updated
✓ Quickly report suspicious messages

🧠 Key Lesson

Initial Access does NOT mean the attacker has already encrypted everything.

It means the attacker has obtained a starting point.

The next stages explain what an attacker might attempt after gaining that foothold — and how defenders can detect and stop the attack before serious impact.
